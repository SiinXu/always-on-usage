Six terms that explain always-on personal agents

@femke_plantingaX ↗Posted 2026-10-29Added 2026-10-29 12:46 UTC♥ 17

What it does
Using an agent that reads your inbox overnight and has a brief ready by morning, the author explains six terms behind Dots, Grok Bot, Muse and Claude Duet: its own cloud computer, integrations (Dots connects to 4,000+ apps), memory, routines (a set time, a new email or a Slack mention), approval gates (Duet asks before it sends, pays, signs or deletes) and an audit log. They note no launch has fully explained whether a task cut off halfway resumes without sending the same email twice, or whether one approval carries over, and suggest testing that before connecting an inbox.

Risks and limits
  • Involves payments, orders or bookings: let the bot draft first and act only after you confirm
  • Reads and writes your mailbox: grant read-only and draft access first, and approve before anything is sent
  • Deletes, unsubscribes or cancels things: get a list first and confirm each item
  • Uses a browser or website login: the costliest kind of step, so prefer a connector where one exists
  • Scheduled runs keep consuming usage: start at a low frequency and raise it once it is stable
  • The author's result may not reproduce for you; the original post is the reference
