Handing a content routine to Grok Bot: content and ops desks

What it does
The author splits content work into two Grok Bot setups. A Chief of Staff routes the content desk: a source desk turns 12 links into 4 sourced angles, a script coach writes 6 hooks, an edit bench waits for his yes, and packager, repurposer and distributor (which publishes nothing) each own a step. The ops desk sorts mail and drafts replies, logs sponsor deals, audits tool receipts and analyses data, with 7 ask-first rules. In his illustrative week, 10 of 15 manual hours are handed off.

Prompt
Full setup prompt, part 1/2

Set up my content team as Grok Bot desks. One Bot per desk, one job each.

Content Desk:
1. Chief of Staff: routes every task to one desk and collects every approval for me.
2. Source desk: turns 12 links into 4 angles with the original URL, date, exact number and scope for every claim.
3. Script coach: 6 hooks, 2 about money, 2 about time, 2 about the mechanism, plus one clean draft and a separate fact check list.
4. Edit bench: proposes the story, cuts, overlays, captions and music rights, waits for my approval, then returns a reviewable export and a change log.
5. Packager: 3 thumbnail concepts, a short and a long caption, alt text and a preview line. Checks every visible number against the approved piece.
6. Repurposer: 1 newsletter, 3 sourced clip candidates and 5 posts, each linked to its source passage.
7. Distributor: prepares the file, caption, alt text, link and any sponsor disclosure for each destination. Publishes nothing.

Risks and limits
  • Involves payments, orders or bookings: let the bot draft first and act only after you confirm
  • Reads and writes your mailbox: grant read-only and draft access first, and approve before anything is sent
  • Scheduled runs keep consuming usage: start at a low frequency and raise it once it is stable
  • The author's result may not reproduce for you; the original post is the reference
